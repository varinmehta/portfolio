I am a student living in Mumbai, India, and a follower of Jainism. While I’ve always respected my religion, my love and connection to it deepened after I joined college. In a world where everything around me felt unfamiliar and new, the one constant I could turn to was my faith.

It started with listening to Bhakti songs, which sparked my curiosity and motivated me to explore Jain teachings more deeply. What I discovered was profound — my religion seemed to hold answers to so many of my questions.

About six to seven months later, I came across YouTube recordings of Perfecting Youth Social sessions. These were far from ordinary preachings. They were crafted with the youth in mind, blending relatable reasoning with timeless teachings. The practicality and logic behind the sermons resonated with me, further strengthening my faith.

## The Practice of Ayambil Tap

Inspired by these teachings, I began practicing Ayambil Tap on random days. Outwardly, I joked that it was my way of losing weight, but inwardly, it was much more profound. My real goal was to gain control over my senses, particularly hunger. Ayambil, which involves eating food devoid of vegetables, fruits, and dairy, felt like a natural detox — both for my body and my mind.

Interestingly, after every Ayambil, I noticed I was about a kilo lighter. This visible change motivated me to continue, and it became a practice I now cherish.

## Redefining My Diet

By mid-2024, I took another step in aligning my diet with my values. One of my favorite foods, bread, was the first to go. While my mom occasionally baked bread without yeast, my previous habit of consuming it frequently seemed unimaginable to give up. But as I stuck with this choice, my cravings naturally declined within 1–1.5 months.

In March 2024, I decided to stop eating Maggi, even the no-onion, no-garlic version. It offered little to no nutritional value, and I realized my body didn’t need it. These changes felt like milestones, reflecting a shift not just in my diet but in my mindset.

A surprising transformation occurred in how I viewed traditional food. A boy who once hated old Gujarati dishes now loves them — a change I could never have anticipated.

## Practicing Discipline and Restraint

Another discipline I’ve been working on for a few years is avoiding food after sunset. While I haven’t fully embraced Chauvihar (no food or water after sunset), I make an effort to follow this principle most days. The idea of reducing harm to microorganisms through night meals inspires me, though my frequent thirst prevents me from committing entirely.

I also refrain from taking pachhakhan (specific vows) because I fear my absent-mindedness may lead to mistakes. For now, my focus is on maintaining a healthy body while reducing harm to all living beings, even microorganisms.

## Loving the Path of Jainism

Looking back, I marvel at how much my relationship with Jainism has grown in just a year. From small practices like avoiding bread and Maggi to larger commitments like Ayambil and no food after sunset, each step has brought me closer to my spiritual goals.

While I don’t yet know the exact force driving these changes, I feel a deep sense of fulfillment and joy in this journey. My ultimate goal is to follow Jainism with utmost sincerity and walk the path of aatma-kalyan (self-liberation). Though the destination is still far, I trust the journey will be extraordinary.
